# Day 3: RTL Design & Synthesis – Optimizations and Constant Propagation

## Overview
On **Day 3**, I explored advanced RTL design concepts using Yosys, with a focus on:
- Constant propagation in circuits
- Optimizations in synthesis
- Handling of D flip-flops (with constant values, resets, sets)
- Technology mapping and gate-level visualization
- Validation of designs through GTKWave

This day provided a deeper understanding of how synthesis tools optimize logic for efficiency and how constant signals simplify circuit design.

---

## 1. Constant Propagation and Optimization

**What I did:**
- Wrote and simulated designs where input constants (`0` or `1`) are propagated through combinational logic.
- Used Yosys to optimize the design, eliminating unnecessary gates.
- Verified optimized results through both synthesis logs and block diagrams.

**Key Observations:**
- When constants are applied, Yosys automatically reduces logic (e.g., AND with 0 → output always 0, OR with 1 → output always 1).
- The optimized netlist is significantly smaller and cleaner.
- Visualization confirmed the simplification.

---

## 2. Optimization Checks (`opt_check` Modules)

- Designed small test modules (`opt_check`, `opt_check2`) to analyze how optimization happens.
- **Results:**  
  - Redundant logic was removed.
  - Only the essential gate remained in the netlist.
  - Verified using block diagrams generated by `show` in Yosys.

---

## 3. D Flip-Flops with Constants

- Explored different variations of **DFFs with constants**:
  1. **DFF with constant input** – Output stabilizes to the constant after the first clock.
  2. **DFF with synchronous reset** – Reset is applied with the clock.
  3. **DFF with asynchronous reset** – Reset takes effect immediately, independent of clock.
  4. **DFF with asynchronous set** – Forces Q = 1 immediately.

**Key Learnings:**
- Yosys mapped these correctly into the **Sky130 standard cell library**.
- Generated netlists showed correct mapping to `dff` cells with reset/set behavior.
- GTKWave confirmed behavior during simulation.

---

## 4. Synthesis and Gate-Level Visualization

- Used `abc -liberty` to map the RTL into technology-specific gates.
- Visualized final netlists with **Graphviz + xdot**.
- Example outcomes:
  - DFF with async reset mapped to **sky130_fd_sc_hd__dfrtp_1** cell.
  - Constant logic modules reduced to minimal gates (`AND`, `OR`) as required.

- **Constant Propagation:** Tools like Yosys automatically optimize designs by removing redundant logic due to constants.
- **Optimization in Synthesis:** Reduces area and power while preserving functionality.
- **DFF Variations:** Learned the practical differences between synchronous vs. asynchronous reset/set and how they are synthesized.
- **Technology Mapping:** Observed how RTL constructs are mapped into actual **Sky130 gates and flip-flops**.
- **Verification Workflow:** Reinforced the use of **GTKWave** + Yosys synthesis flow to validate RTL → Gate-Level → Waveform.
